What is exponential growth?

It's when something grows by a percentage of itself each step, instead of by the same fixed amount every time, so the bigger it gets, the bigger each step's increase gets too.

Doubling means the increase itself keeps growing

Going from 1 to 2 adds one. Going from 16 to 32 adds thirty-two. Same doubling rule, much bigger jump, because it's applied to a much bigger number.

Looks slow, then suddenly isn't

Bacteria that double every minute fill an empty bottle in exactly one hour. Drag the slider. Watch how long it stays looking almost empty.

Why this trick works on almost anyone

Physicist Albert Bartlett used this exact bottle to argue that people misjudge exponential growth (a population, a debt, a resource running out) because most of the curve looks calm right up until the last couple of doublings, and by then there's no time left to react.

Nothing grows like this forever

A resource-limited population, a spreading illness, a market (anything real) runs into a ceiling: not enough food, not enough susceptible people left, not enough buyers. Growth slows and flattens instead of climbing forever.

Sources: Albert A. Bartlett, "Arithmetic, Population and Energy" (lecture transcript). "45.3 Environmental Limits to Population Growth," Biology 2e, OpenStax. Longer, more technical version: Reference: Exponential Growth.
