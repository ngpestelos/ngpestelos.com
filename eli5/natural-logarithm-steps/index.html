What is a natural logarithm?

ln(x) gives the step number on a continuous scale where each whole step multiplies by e.

One full step multiplies by e.

Starting at 1, one full step gives e ≈ 2.718, and two full steps give e2 ≈ 7.389.

What a “step” means
  • A step number s is a real exponent: x = es.
  • Euler’s number e begins 2.718281828….

ln reads the step number.

ln(x) returns the exponent s in es = x.

Slider range and rounding
  • The slider covers 0.20 to 8.00 in increments of 0.01.
  • The point uses ln(x) before rounding; the readouts show two and three decimal places.
  • The slider may not land exactly on e.

Half a step uses √e.

Two half-steps multiply by √e twice, giving one full factor of e.

Why half a step is a square root
  • e0.5 = √e.
  • √e × √e = e, so two moves of 0.5 give a move of 1.
  • A fractional step is a real exponent, not a count of whole multiplications.

Going back one step divides by e.

A positive value below 1 has a negative step number because e raised to a negative exponent is below 1.

Negative steps and the input limit
  • e−1 = 1/e ≈ 0.368, so ln(1/e) = −1.
  • es stays positive for every real step number s.
  • The real natural logarithm therefore accepts only x > 0.

Sources: NIST DLMF, §4.2: Definitions; OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §1.5: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions.
