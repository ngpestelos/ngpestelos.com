How Does an AI Answer Things It Wasn't Taught?

The fancy term is RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The idea underneath is one you already use: look it up before you answer.

The big word, unpacked

RAG is three steps glued together: find the right document, add it to what the AI is reading, then let it write the answer.

The problem: its memory has a closing date

An AI only knows what existed before its training stopped. Ask about anything after that date, and it either fails — or answers anyway, confidently, from a guess.

Why it doesn't just say "I don't know"

An AI writes one likely next word at a time. There is no built-in switch that says "stop, I have nothing here" — the most likely next word is still a confident-sounding one, even when nothing behind it is true.

The fix: look it up, then answer

RAG hands the AI real documents at the moment you ask, so it can read an answer instead of inventing one.

The library gets built ahead of time

Before any question shows up, the documents get chopped into small pieces and filed by what they mean, so the right piece can be found fast, later.

It can still grab the wrong page

The lookup step finds pages that sound like the question, not always the page that answers it.

And finding the right page still isn't a guarantee

Even when the lookup grabs the exact right page, the AI can still write an answer that contradicts it. Two different things have to go right: finding the correct source, and actually being faithful to it once it's found.
