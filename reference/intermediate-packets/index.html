Intermediate Packets

Reference entry · last updated September 4, 2026

Intermediate Packet is a reusable piece of work that can contribute to a current or future project. Tiago Forte introduced the term.[1] In his Building a Second Brain system, Intermediate Packets include tangible units such as notes, outlines, drafts, slides, templates, and completed deliverables.[3]

Origin and purpose

Forte used the term in his February 2017 article “Bending the Curves of Productivity.” He proposed packaging research, notes, brainstorms, examples, outlines, prototypes, drafts, and unused ideas as reusable components.[1]

Three months later, Forte described Intermediate Packets as units smaller than deliverables, including digital notes, work-in-progress drafts, outlines, brainstorms, and checklists.[2] The Building a Second Brain book places these reusable assets in the Express stage of its Capture, Organize, Distill, Express method.[3]

Five kinds of Intermediate Packets

Building a Second Brain identifies five kinds of packets.[3]

Distilled notes

Distilled notes compress books, articles, meetings, or research into material that can be reviewed and applied without rereading the full source.

Outtakes

Outtakes are useful material removed from an earlier project. A cut paragraph, unused diagram, or rejected example can remain available for later work.

Work-in-process

Work-in-process includes artifacts created while a project develops, such as plans, agendas, graphics, requirement documents, checklists, and drafts.

Final deliverables

Completed reports, presentations, templates, and other delivered work can supply parts of later projects.

Documents created by others

Documents from colleagues, clients, contractors, and other contributors can serve as packets when permission, attribution, and reuse rights allow it.

Practical benefits

Intermediate Packets reduce the amount of project state held in memory. A preserved artifact provides a clear point for resuming work after an interruption.[1]

  • Short work periods: A packet can fit the time available.
  • Earlier feedback: A small draft or prototype can be reviewed before the project is heavily committed.
  • Lower starting cost: Existing research, outlines, and templates reduce blank-page work.
  • Reuse: One artifact can contribute to several deliverables or projects.

Relationship to atomic notes

An atomic note is classified by conceptual scope. It centers on one idea or knowledge building block.[4] An Intermediate Packet is classified by reuse in project work. Its size and format depend on the work it supports.

The categories overlap when an atomic note becomes input to a project. Meeting notes, slide decks, drafts, templates, and final deliverables may contain several ideas while remaining reusable units of work.[3] The Zettelkasten atomicity principle and Forte's Intermediate Packet concept address different properties of an artifact.[5]
