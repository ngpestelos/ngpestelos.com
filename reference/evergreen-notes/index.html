Evergreen Notes

Reference entry · last updated August 30, 2026

Evergreen notes are self-contained, concept-oriented digital notes designed to be continually updated, accumulated, and interconnected over years of research and writing.[1] Formulated by researcher Andy Matuschak, the evergreen note methodology adapts the principles of the analog Zettelkasten to modern digital hypertext and digital gardening.[2]

Core principles

The evergreen note methodology is defined by four core operational principles:[1]

  • Atomic: Each note captures exactly one distinct concept. This modularity prevents ideas from being trapped inside sprawling long-form documents.
  • Concept-oriented: Notes are organized around abstract ideas and mechanisms rather than specific books, authors, projects, or calendar dates.
  • Densely linked: Notes link bidirectionally to related concepts. Links represent explicit semantic relationships rather than passive tag categories.
  • Continually revised: Rather than treating notes as static archival records, the author returns to existing notes to refine explanations, add counterarguments, and integrate new evidence over time.

Titles as declarative claims

A key innovation of evergreen note-taking is phrasing note titles as complete declarative sentences.[3] Instead of using broad topical labels like Memory and Learning or Spaced Repetition, an evergreen note title states a specific proposition, such as Spaced repetition memory systems can be used to develop conceptual understanding.

Matuschak compares declarative note titles to software APIs (Application Programming Interfaces). When writing future essays or notes, the author embeds these precise propositions as linked references, allowing complex arguments to be composed from modular, verified sub-claims.[3]

Relationship to Zettelkasten permanent notes

Evergreen notes share direct intellectual lineage with the permanent notes of Niklas Luhmann's Zettelkasten system, popularized by Sönke Ahrens in How to Take Smart Notes.[4] Both frameworks emphasize atomicity, personal synthesis, and non-hierarchical organization.

However, evergreen notes reflect digital affordances that Luhmann lacked in paper index cards: notes can be edited in place without rewriting physical cards, backlinks reveal incoming references automatically, and titles replace manual alphanumeric branching sequence numbers.

Digital gardens and public note curation

Evergreen note-taking catalyzed the modern "digital garden" movement.[5] In contrast to chronological blogs or static personal wikis, a digital garden publishes unpolished, work-in-progress thinking organized by topological links. Readers navigate through ideas via associative discovery rather than reverse-chronological feeds.
