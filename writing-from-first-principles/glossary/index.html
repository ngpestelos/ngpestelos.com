6. Glossary

One definition each, in the sense the chapters use. Alphabetical.

  • Chain (linear, hub). The two shapes a paragraph's middle can take. Linear: each sentence picks up the end of the one before. Hub: the first sentence's subject stays the subject of every supporting sentence. A paragraph picks one.
  • Claim. One sentence, with a verb, that a reasonable reader could deny. A topic is not a claim.
  • Cost. What the reader keeps doing, paying, or believing wrongly if they ignore the claim. Claim plus cost is the thesis.
  • Delete-only pass. A revision that removes words and adds none. The trim at essay scale, the cut at sentence scale.
  • Evidence. One specific thing that answers "how do you know": a number, an incident, a quote, a file, a date. An adjective is not evidence.
  • Hand-off. The last sentence of a paragraph, doing one job: a consequence, a bridge to the next first sentence, or nothing.
  • Kill-word. See operator.
  • Known-first, new-last. The one rule at every scale. Open on what the reader already holds. Close on what they must carry forward.
  • Map. The pre-read pass over first sentences, headings, and tables that builds slots before the details arrive. Written as a guessed claim, the proofs needed, and a prior view.
  • Mechanism. The sentence that answers "why does that follow": the causal link between the evidence and the claim. The sentence writers skip.
  • Mode (mastery, exposure, locating). Why you are reading, and so how. Mastery: full read, model built, objections written. Exposure: first sentences only, one line, stop. Locating: scan for the target, read that paragraph only.
  • Nominalization. A verb turned into a noun: decision, failure, implementation, analysis. It hides the action and drops the actor. The repair is to turn it back and ask who did it.
  • Objection. The "but" a skeptical reader raises. Each one is a paragraph. The body of an essay is the objections in the order they arise.
  • Operator. A small word that flips or bounds a claim rather than adding detail: not, only, except, unless, additional, excluding, must, cannot, however. Skipping one leaves a model that is wrong in a way that feels right.
  • Production test. Without the page: the claim, two supports, one objection, in your own words, under a minute. The only test of comprehension.
  • Slot. A place in the reader's working model for a detail to land. Built by the map. A detail with no slot falls out within a sentence.
  • Spine. The actor and the action of a sentence: a subject that can act, a verb it can do. Everything else hangs from it.
  • Thesis. See cost.
  • Topic sentence. The first sentence of a paragraph, stated as a claim. Read only these and the argument should be complete.
