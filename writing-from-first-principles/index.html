Writing from First Principles

Improve the reader's own reading and writing to lessen dependency on large language models.

The guide is written from first principles. It starts from eight facts about how people read. One example: a reader can stop at any sentence. The facts force one rule, and every test in the chapters follows from that rule. When a rule seems arbitrary, find the fact behind it.

  • 0. The Reader. Eight facts, one rule, the case file, the no-model protocol.
  • 1. Reading Comprehension. Build slots, fill them, reproduce, object.
  • 2. Essay Structure. Claim and cost, the objection chain, opening and close.
  • 3. Paragraph Construction. First sentence, two questions, hand-off.
  • 4. Sentence Construction. Spine, known-first new-last, seven cuts.
  • 5. Practice Card. The twelve tests on one page.
  • 6. Glossary. The terms, one definition each.

Start at The Reader. Each chapter ends with an exercise on your own writing from the last week.
